Reference ontology

What chemical engineering covers — and where this course fits

This is a map of what you are expected to know by the time you graduate as a chemical engineer: 16 knowledge areas, broken into units and topics, drawn from the PEC 2023–2024 accreditation guidelines alongside AIChE/ABET programme criteria. No single course covers it — each one you take fills in a part of it. It is kept separate from any course syllabus on purpose, so it stays a map of the discipline rather than a list of what happens to be taught this term.

CHE-226 lives in KA-01, but the notebooks do not walk this map one topic at a time. Each notebook teaches a piece of Python — variables, loops, functions, arrays — and then takes the examples that carry it from wherever they fit here: a density calculation, an energy balance, the Reynolds number for water in a pipe, an Arrhenius rate constant. One notebook therefore turns up against several topics below, and one topic can be reached by several notebooks. There is no plan to write a separate notebook per topic, and a topic with no notebook against it is not a gap in your Python.

Use it to place what you are doing. When you write a function for the Arrhenius equation in T4, the chip on that topic tells you the same equation is core to Reaction Engineering later; the loop you write over temperature readings is the arithmetic behind an energy balance. Knowing where something lands is most of what makes it stay.

How to read this page

Every topic can carry chips telling you which notebook to open:

  • T4 · Functions ↗ the notebook that teaches this topic — this is course material.
  • T4 · Functions · example ↗ a notebook whose worked example uses this topic. The engineering is the vehicle, the Python is the lesson — but the calculation is real, and you will meet it again.
  • not covered this term nothing in CHE-226 reaches it. Still part of your degree, and still worth reading.

Most topics carry no chip at all, which is normal — this is the whole discipline, and one programming course touches a corner of it. Search below to find a topic, use Core units only to hide the electives, and open an area to see the units and topics inside it.
