Reference ontology

What each lecture covers, on the map of chemical engineering

The reference ontology is a map of what you are expected to know by the time you graduate as a chemical engineer: 16 knowledge areas, broken into units and topics, drawn from the PEC 2023–2024 accreditation guidelines alongside AIChE/ABET programme criteria. It is kept separate from any course syllabus on purpose, so it stays a map of the discipline.

Each CHE-226 lecture teaches a piece of Python, which sits in KA-01, and carries it with worked examples borrowed from across the map: the Reynolds number in T3, the Arrhenius equation in T4, heat transfer resistances in T7, capital cost estimation in T15. Those examples are previews of courses still ahead of you. The first section below lists, lecture by lecture, the topics each one touches; the full ontology after it shows every topic, as a list or as a graph.

How to read this page

In the full ontology below, a topic can carry chips telling you which lecture to open:

  • T4 · Functions ↗ the notebook that teaches this topic — this is course material.
  • T4 · Functions · example ↗ a notebook whose worked example uses this topic. The engineering is the vehicle, the Python is the lesson — but the calculation is real, and you will meet it again.
  • not covered this term a KA-01 topic nothing in CHE-226 reaches. Still part of your degree, and still worth reading.

Most topics carry no chip at all, which is normal — this is the whole discipline, and one programming course touches a corner of it. Search below to find a topic, open the graph view to see the whole map at once, use Core units only to hide the electives, and open an area to see the units and topics inside it.
